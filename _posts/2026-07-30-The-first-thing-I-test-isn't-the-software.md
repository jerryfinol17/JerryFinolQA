When I first started in QA, I believed testing started the moment you opened a website.

Click every button.
Open every menu.
Verify every link.
Try to break everything.
That was my process.
Or at least...

I thought it was.

## One day I opened a website and found myself completely lost.

Not because it was confusing.
Because I didn't understand what it was.

I remember thinking:

"I can test every button on this page... but I still don't understand what this product is."

So I stopped.
Made myself a cup of coffee.
And started asking questions instead.

What is this?
Who built it?
Why does it exist?
What problem is it trying to solve?

A few minutes later...
Everything clicked.
It wasn't a SaaS platform.
It wasn't an application.

It was the official website **for** a product.

That changed everything.

## Suddenly, I Was Asking Different Questions

Instead of asking:

"Does every button work?"

I started asking:

Does this website explain the product clearly?
Does it build trust?
Does it make me curious?
Does it make me want the product?
Would I keep reading?

Because that's the real purpose of a product website.
Not existing.
Converting visitors into customers.

## Only after understanding that...

I started the technical review.

UI.
UX.
Accessibility.
Responsiveness.
Broken links.
Forms.
Edge cases.
Performance.
Everything.

But now...
Every technical finding had context.

## That's When My Reports Changed

Before...
My reports answered technical questions.

Now...
They answer business questions too.

Questions like:

Where did I feel confused?
What made me trust the product?
Where did I hesitate?
What convinced me to continue?
Would I recommend it?

Those aren't bug reports.
They're user insights.
And founders have told me those observations are often just as valuable as the technical findings.

## Final Reflection

Today, before writing my first bug...

I try to understand the idea.

Because if I don't understand what a founder is trying to build...

I can't honestly tell them whether they're succeeding.

The first thing I test isn't the software.

It's the idea.
